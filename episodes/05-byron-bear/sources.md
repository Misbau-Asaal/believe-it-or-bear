# 05 Byron's Bear: sources and fact notes

- University of Cambridge, "Lord Byron and the bears beneath Cambridge": https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/lord-byron-and-the-bears-beneath-cambridge
- The Wordsworth Trust, "Bears, Badgers and Boatswain: Lord Byron and his Animals": https://wordsworth.org.uk/blog/2015/04/21/bears-badgers-and-boatswain-lord-byron-and-his-animals/
- Byron Bytes, "Trinity College, Cambridge, October 1805 - July 1808": https://byron-bytes.blogspot.com/2026/01/trinity-college-cambridge-october-1805.html
- Byron's letters (letter to Elizabeth Pigot, 26 October 1807), Letters and Journals vol. 1: https://archive.org/download/letters18041813e00byrouoft/letters18041813e00byrouoft.pdf

- Byron arrived at Trinity College, Cambridge in October 1805 (Byron Bytes).
- He loved animals: he kept dogs all his life and later a menagerie at Newstead Abbey (Wordsworth Trust).
- College rules forbade students to keep dogs, and said nothing about bears (Wordsworth Trust, Cambridge). The rule itself is reported, not quoted from a statute, so the script says "the college rules were clear: no dogs" without quoting any wording. The other lines on the notice board in the video are background decoration.
- He got a tame bear; the Cambridge article says he "is said to have purchased" it, "quite possibly at Stourbridge Fair". The script just says "bought"; the fair stall is a dramatization.
- He walked it on a chain like a dog (Byron Bytes).
- In his letter of 26 October 1807: "I have got a new friend, the finest in the world, a tame bear. When I brought him here, they asked me what I meant to do with him, and my reply was, 'he should sit for a fellowship.'" The script quotes the reply exactly. "They" is not named in the letter; the script says "the officials" (college authorities, as most retellings have it).
- "Sit for a fellowship": take the examination to become a fellow (a senior member who teaches) of the college; the script explains it as "take the exam to teach at Cambridge".
- Nobody claims the bear actually sat an exam; the exam hall at the end illustrates the joke. Afterwards the bear went with Byron to Newstead Abbey (Wordsworth Trust).
- Mortarboards and gowns are drawn in a simple, generic style; no real person is shown besides Byron, drawn as our own cartoon.
