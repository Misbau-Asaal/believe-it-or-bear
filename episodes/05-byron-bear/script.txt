Lord Byron's college banned dogs. So he brought a bear.

In 1805, the young poet arrived at Trinity College, Cambridge.

He loved animals. But the college rules were clear: no dogs.

Byron read them again. Dogs? Banned. Bears? Not a word.

So he bought a tame bear.

He walked it around the college on a chain, just like a dog.

Students stared. Then the officials asked Byron what he meant to do with it.

He had his answer ready.

"He should sit for a fellowship."

In other words, the bear should take the exam to teach at Cambridge.
