# 04 The Microwave: sources and fact notes

- Wikipedia, "Percy Spencer": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer
- Lemelson-MIT, "Percy Spencer": https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer
- APS News, "October 8, 1945: First Patent for Microwave Oven": https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2015/10/first-patent-for-microwave
- New England Historical Society, "Percy Spencer Melts a Chocolate Bar, Invents the Microwave Oven": https://newenglandhistoricalsociety.com/percy-spencer-melts-chocolate-bar-invents-microwave-oven/

- Spencer was a self-taught radar engineer (he never finished grammar school) who worked on mass-producing magnetrons, the tubes that power radar.
- The melted candy bar near a running magnetron is how the discovery is traditionally told (Wikipedia: "according to legend"), so the script opens with "Legend says". No brand is named in any source, so none is shown.
- Popcorn kernels held near the magnetron popped (Lemelson-MIT, APS, NEHS).
- An egg in a kettle exploded in the face of a coworker who was looking in (Wikipedia, APS).
- The cooking patent was filed on 8 October 1945 (APS). Its title, "Method of Treating Foodstuffs", is on the certificate in the office shot.
- The first commercial microwave oven weighed about 750 pounds (every source agrees). Its height (5 ft 6 in to 6 ft) and price ($2,000 to $5,000) vary by source, so the script gives only the weight.
- Spencer received no royalties, only a one-time $2 gratuity, the company's standard payment for an employee's patent (Wikipedia, NEHS). The script says "bonus".
- "Shake the water in food": microwaves make the water molecules in food rotate back and forth, and that motion becomes heat (simplified for the script).
