# 06 Napoleon vs the Rabbits: sources and fact notes

- Mental Floss, "The Time Napoleon Was Attacked by Rabbits": https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/51364/time-napoleon-was-attacked-rabbits
- The Star (Malaysia), "Quickcheck: Did a hunt cause Napoleon Bonaparte to retreat due to rabbits?" (5 Sep 2024): https://www.thestar.com.my/news/true-or-not/2024/09/05/quickcheck-did-a-hunt-cause-napoleon-bonaparte-to-retreat-due-to-rabbits
- Primary source: the memoirs of General Paul Thiébault (French edition published 1895; English translation on archive.org), as cited by both articles above.

- The script frames it as "Legend says", because the whole account rests mainly on one memoir published decades later (The Star notes this).
- 1807, after the Treaties of Tilsit (July 1807); the hunt was to celebrate the peace (Mental Floss, The Star). The video shows a treaty being signed in a tent as a dramatization.
- Berthier, Napoleon's chief of staff, organised it (both sources).
- Numbers vary: "hundreds" up to 3,000 (Mental Floss); The Star says a thousand. The script says "hundreds", the low end.
- The rabbits ran at Napoleon instead of away (both sources).
- Berthier had bought tame rabbits from local farmers instead of catching wild ones, and they apparently took people for whoever fed them (Mental Floss). The script says "To them, people meant dinner."
- Men waved sticks and the coachmen cracked whips (Mental Floss).
- The horde splitting into two wings and coming round both sides is from historian David Chandler's retelling (Mental Floss); Thiébault says they "turned the Emperor's flank".
- Napoleon retreated to his carriage (both sources); some rabbits reportedly leapt in, and the attack stopped when it drove off (Mental Floss).
- Dramatizations: the wild rabbit and its burrow, the farm with "TAME RABBITS" hutches, the rumbling tummy and carrot thought bubble, the rabbit knocking off and wearing Napoleon's hat, the 1807 banner, and the chateau in the background.
